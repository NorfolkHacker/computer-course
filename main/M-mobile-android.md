## The Operator says

Luser list, entry thirteen: "The phone has no buttons." It hid its buttons to look sleek and replaced them with secret finger-waving. Very modern.

Your phone is a small computer that goes everywhere with you, which makes it the computer most likely to be lost, dropped or stolen. So learn the four or five moves that get you around, get the apps from the one safe shop, and lock the thing.

## The Mender's way

Android phones (Samsung, Google Pixel, Motorola and others) all work the same way, but menu names vary by maker. Google Pixel wording is given here; if you can't find something, open **Settings** and type its name into the search bar at the top.

### The parts of the screen

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Lock screen** | What you see when the phone wakes up: the time, and notifications. |
| **Home screen** | The main screen, with a few app icons and usually a Google search bar. Swipe sideways for more. |
| **App** | A program on the phone, shown as a small picture (an **icon**). Tap once to open it. |
| **App drawer** | The full list of every app on the phone. Swipe **up** from the middle of the home screen to see it. |

### Getting around: gestures or three buttons

Look at the very bottom of the screen.

**If there's a thin line** at the bottom, you have **gesture navigation**:

- **Go home:** swipe up from the bottom edge.
- **Go back:** swipe in from the left or right edge of the screen, towards the middle.
- **See recent apps:** swipe up from the bottom, hold a moment, let go. Tap the one you want.

**If there are three symbols** at the bottom, you have **three-button navigation**:

- **◀ Back**: goes back one step.
- **● Home**: goes to the home screen.
- **■ Recent apps**: shows apps you've used lately. (On some Samsung phones the order is reversed.)

To switch between them, open **Settings** and search for `navigation mode` (on a Pixel: **System** → **Navigation mode**; on a Samsung: **Display** → **Navigation bar**).

### Notifications and quick settings

1. Swipe **down** from the top of the screen. This is the **notification shade**: messages, missed calls and alerts from apps. Tap one to open it; swipe it sideways to dismiss it.
2. Swipe **down again**. This opens **Quick Settings**: tiles you tap to switch things on and off, such as **Wi-Fi**, **Bluetooth**, **Torch** and **Aeroplane mode**, and a slider for screen brightness.
3. Swipe up to put it away.

### The buttons on the side

- **Volume up / down**: press to change the volume. A slider appears on the screen.
- **Power** (or **side**) button: a **short press** locks the phone and turns the screen off; another short press wakes it.
- **Switching off or restarting:** on many phones, holding the power button opens the assistant (like in **What went wrong** above). Instead, swipe down twice from the top and tap the **power** icon ⏻ in Quick Settings, then **Power off** or **Restart**. On a Pixel you can also press **Power** and **Volume up** together.

### Installing apps: only from the Play Store

The **Google Play Store** (a coloured triangle icon) is the official shop for Android apps. Google checks them, and **Play Protect** scans them.

1. Open **Play Store**.
2. Tap **Search** at the bottom and type the app's name.
3. Before installing, check the name of the company underneath it (for example, the Gmail app should say **Google LLC**), the star rating and the number of downloads. Fakes often have a slightly wrong name and few downloads.
4. Tap **Install**. When it's done, tap **Open**, or find it in the app drawer.

Never install an app from a link in a text, email or website. If your phone asks whether to **allow installs from unknown sources**, say no.

### Locking your screen

A **screen lock** means the phone needs your PIN, pattern, fingerprint or face to unlock, so if it's lost nobody can get into your email, photos or bank.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Pixel: tap **Security & privacy**, then **Device unlock**, then **Screen lock**. Samsung: tap **Lock screen and AOD** (or **Lock screen**), then **Screen lock and biometrics** (or **Screen lock type**). Anywhere else: search for `screen lock`.
3. Choose **PIN** (at least six digits, not your birthday) and follow the steps.
4. Optionally add a **fingerprint** or **face unlock**; the PIN stays as the backup.

## Do it now

1. Swipe up to open the app drawer and find the **Clock** app. Open it.
2. Go back to the home screen (swipe up from the bottom, or press **●**).
3. Open **Recent apps** and switch back to **Clock**.
4. Swipe down twice to open Quick Settings and turn the **Torch** on and off.
5. Check your phone has a screen lock: press the power button once, then again. Does it ask for a PIN, pattern, fingerprint or face?

You've done it when you've moved between home, apps and Quick Settings without guessing, and your phone asks for something before it unlocks.

## Quiz

```quiz
  options:
    - Tap the line hard
    - Swipe in from the left or right edge of the screen
    - Hold the power button
  answer: 1
  why: With gesture navigation, swiping in from either side edge goes back. Holding power often opens the assistant instead.
- q: Where should you install Android apps from?
  options:
    - A link someone texted you
    - Any website with a download button
    - The Google Play Store
  answer: 2
  why: The Play Store checks its apps and Play Protect scans them. Apps from links and websites are a common way phones get infected.
- q: How do you open Quick Settings (Wi-Fi, torch, brightness)?
  options:
    - Swipe down from the top, then down again
    - Swipe up from the bottom
    - Press both volume buttons
  answer: 0
  why: The first swipe down shows notifications; the second shows the Quick Settings tiles.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble makes fake apps, like a torch app that wants your contacts and messages. When a new app asks for **permissions** (to see your contacts, messages, location), ask yourself if it needs them. A torch doesn't need your contacts. Tap **Don't allow**, then uninstall the app.
