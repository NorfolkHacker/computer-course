## The Operator says

Luser list, entry three: forty-one copies of one spreadsheet open, and a complaint that the computer is "slow". It was tired.

Everything happens inside a **window**, and every window can be hidden, enlarged or closed. Know which button does which, and where hidden windows go, and you stop losing things that were never lost.

## The Mender's way

The screen you see after signing in is the **desktop**. The bar along the bottom is the **taskbar**. Apps open in rectangles called **windows** that sit on top of the desktop.

### The Start menu: opening apps

1. Click the **Start** button: the Windows logo ⊞ in the taskbar at the bottom of the screen. You can also press the <kbd>Windows</kbd> key on the keyboard.
2. The **Start menu** opens. At the top is a search box; under it, **Pinned** shows some apps as icons.
3. To open an app, click its icon once.
4. If the app isn't in **Pinned**, either type its name straight away (the search box fills in as you type) and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>, or click **All** (or **All apps**, depending on your version) at the top right of **Pinned** to see an A–Z list.

### The three window buttons

Every window has three buttons, **top right**:

| Button | Looks like | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimise** | A short line – | Hides the window in the taskbar. It's still open. |
| **Maximise** | A square □ | Makes the window fill the whole screen. When it's full screen, the button becomes **Restore** (two overlapping squares), which shrinks it back. |
| **Close** | A cross ✕ | Closes the app. If work is unsaved, it asks first. |

To move a window, drag it by its **title bar** (the strip along the top, where its name is). To resize it, drag an edge or corner.

### The taskbar: what's open

Each open app has an icon on the taskbar. An app that's open has a **short line under its icon**; the app you're using right now has a longer, brighter line.

- To bring back a minimised window, click its icon on the taskbar once.
- If an app's icon already has a line under it, it's open: click that instead of opening it again.

### Switching between windows

1. Click the app's icon on the taskbar, **or**
2. Hold <kbd>Alt</kbd>, press <kbd>Tab</kbd> once, and keep holding <kbd>Alt</kbd>. Small pictures of every open window appear. Press <kbd>Tab</kbd> again to move along; let go of <kbd>Alt</kbd> to switch to the highlighted one.

### Pinning an app to the taskbar

**Pinning** keeps an app's icon on the taskbar even when it's closed.

1. Open **Start** and find the app (type its name).
2. Right-click it.
3. Click **Pin to taskbar**.

To remove one, right-click its icon on the taskbar and click **Unpin from taskbar**. The app itself stays on the computer.

### On Android (your phone)

The phone has the same ideas with different names. This is the Google/Pixel way; Samsung is very similar.

- **Home screen**: the phone's desktop, with app icons. Press the home button or swipe up from the very bottom edge to get back to it.
- **All apps** (the "app drawer"): swipe up from the middle of the home screen to see every app. Tap one to open it.
- **Switching apps** (like Alt + Tab): swipe up from the bottom edge, hold for a second, and let go; or tap the square ▢ or three-lines button at the bottom if your phone has buttons. Swipe sideways through the cards and tap the one you want.
- **Closing an app**: in that view, swipe its card up off the screen. You rarely need to.
- **Pinning** (putting an app on the home screen): in **All apps**, touch and hold the app's icon, then drag it up onto the home screen and let go.

## Do it now

1. Open **Notepad** from the **Start** menu.
2. Click **Maximise** so it fills the screen, then **Restore** to shrink it back.
3. Click **Minimise**. Notice the Notepad icon on the taskbar still has a line under it. Click that icon to bring the same window back.
4. Open **Settings** from the **Start** menu as well. Use <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> to switch back to Notepad, then to Settings.
5. Right-click the **Settings** icon on the taskbar and click **Pin to taskbar** (if it says **Unpin from taskbar**, it's already pinned; leave it).
6. Close both windows with their ✕ buttons. If Notepad asks to save, click **Don't save**.

You've done it when you hid a window and got the *same* window back, without opening a second copy.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: You clicked the short line (–) in a window's corner and the window disappeared. Where is it?
  options:
    - It's closed and your work is lost
    - It's minimised; click its icon on the taskbar to bring it back
    - It's in the Recycle Bin
  answer: 1
  why: Minimise only hides the window in the taskbar. The app is still open, with your work in it.
- q: How can you tell an app is already open before you open it again?
  options:
    - Its taskbar icon has a short line under it
    - Its icon is on the desktop
    - The computer goes slower
  answer: 0
  why: A line under a taskbar icon means that app is open. Click it to go back to it instead of opening another copy.
- q: You use the Calculator every day. What's the quickest way to keep it one click away?
  options:
    - Leave it open all the time, minimised
    - Search for it from scratch every time
    - Open it several times so there's always one ready
    - Right-click it in Start and choose Pin to taskbar
  answer: 3
  why: Pinning keeps its icon on the taskbar even when it's closed, so one click opens it.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble likes to make windows that *look* like Windows: a box with a red warning, a fake ✕, and a message saying your computer is infected. Habit: real Windows messages don't fill your web browser with flashing warnings and phone numbers. If one appears, don't click anything inside it and don't ring the number. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>W</kbd> to close the tab, or close the whole browser from its taskbar icon (right-click, **Close window**). Lesson J has more.
