## The Operator says

At last. My lesson. "Have you tried turning it off and on again?" I've said it ten thousand times and it has worked nine thousand of them. I want it on my headstone.

It's not a joke. It's the most powerful repair tool you own. But there's an order to things. Close the one stuck app before you pull the plug on the whole computer. And when you ask for help, give the helper something better than "a box". I can fix almost anything. I can't fix "it did a thing".

## The Mender's way

Most problems have a simple fix. Work through these in order, starting with the gentlest.

### Why turning it off and on again works

A computer keeps what it's working on in its **memory** (Lesson A). Over time a program can get muddled, stuck waiting for something that will never happen. Switching off empties the memory; switching on starts everything fresh. It also finishes half-installed updates.

### 1. Close the stuck app

If one app has frozen (it says **Not Responding**, or ignores clicks), wait a minute; it may be busy. If it's still stuck:

### On Windows (Task Manager)

1. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Esc</kbd>. **Task Manager** opens: a list of everything that's running.
2. On the **Processes** page, click once on the frozen app's name.
3. Click **End task** (at the top of the window).
4. The app closes. Open it again from **Start**.

Unsaved work may be lost, though Word and many apps offer to recover it when reopened.

### On Android (Force stop)

1. Long-press (touch and hold) the app's icon.
2. Tap **App info** (or the **ⓘ** icon).
3. Tap **Force stop**, then **OK**.
4. Open the app again.

### 2. Restart the device

If more than one thing is misbehaving, or closing the app didn't help, restart.

### On Windows (Restart)

1. Save anything you can.
2. Click **Start**, then the **power** icon ⏻, then **Restart**.

Use **Restart**, not **Shut down**, when fixing problems; it gives the cleanest fresh start. Only if the computer is completely frozen for several minutes, hold the power button for about 10 seconds (Lesson A).

### On Android (Restart)

1. Press and hold the **power button** on the side. On newer Pixels hold **power** and **volume up** together instead; on Samsung, hold the **side key** and **volume down** together (for a few seconds; a quick press takes a screenshot).
2. Tap **Restart** on the menu that appears.

You can also swipe down twice from the top of the screen and tap the **power** icon ⏻.

### 3. Check the simple things

- **Cables:** is everything plugged in at both ends, and switched on at the wall? Push each plug in firmly.
- **Wi-Fi:** look at the bottom-right of the taskbar. A globe with a line through it means no internet. Click it and check Wi-Fi is on and connected (Lesson G). On the phone, swipe down from the top and check the Wi-Fi tile.
- **Is it just you?** If one website won't load, try another. If none load, restart your **router** (the internet box): off, wait 30 seconds, on, then give it five minutes.

### 4. Read the error message

An **error message** is a box telling you what went wrong. Don't click **OK** straight away. Read it slowly. Often it tells you the fix: "No internet connection", "Disk full", "Printer is out of paper".

Write the exact words down, or take a screenshot with <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd> (Lesson Q). On the phone, press **power** and **volume down** together.

### 5. Search for the exact message

Search for the exact words of the message, in quotation marks, with the app's name. For example: `"Word found unreadable content" Windows 11`. Somebody has almost always had the same problem. Prefer the company's own support site (**support.microsoft.com**, **support.google.com**). Never follow an answer that tells you to download a "fix tool" from an unknown site.

### 6. Asking for help well

When you ask someone for help, tell them:

1. **What you were doing**: "I was printing a letter from Word."
2. **What you expected**: "I expected one page to come out."
3. **What happened instead**: "Nothing came out, and Word froze."
4. **The exact message**, word for word.
5. **A screenshot**, if you can.
6. **What you've already tried**: "I restarted the computer and the printer."

With those six things, almost anyone can help you quickly.

## Do it now

1. Open Notepad (**Start**, type `Notepad`).
2. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Esc</kbd> to open Task Manager.
3. Find **Notepad** in the list, click it once, and click **End task**. Notepad closes. Close Task Manager.
4. On your phone, open any app, then use **App info** and **Force stop** to close it.
5. Restart your phone using the power menu.

You've done it when Notepad vanished from Task Manager and your phone has restarted and is asking for your PIN.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: Word says "Not Responding" and won't do anything. What's the best first step after waiting a minute?
  options:
    - Hold the computer's power button down
    - Ctrl + Shift + Esc, select Word, then End task
    - Click everywhere very fast
  answer: 1
  why: Task Manager closes just the stuck app and leaves everything else running. Holding the power button is only for when the whole computer is frozen.
- q: An error message pops up. What should you do with it?
  options:
    - Click OK straight away to make it go away
    - Read it, and write down or screenshot the exact words
    - Ignore it, it's just a box
  answer: 1
  why: The message often says what's wrong and how to fix it, and the exact words are what you search for or give to a helper.
- q: Which of these is a good way to ask for help?
  options:
    - "It's broken"
    - "It said a thing, a box"
    - "I was printing from Word, expected one page, got nothing; the message said 'Printer offline'; I've restarted both"
  answer: 2
  why: Saying what you did, what you expected, what happened and the exact message lets the helper fix it quickly.
```

## Scribble watch

When something breaks, the Scribble pretends to help: a pop-up with a "Microsoft Support" phone number, or a stranger offering to "fix it remotely". Microsoft never puts a phone number in a warning pop-up. Never ring numbers from pop-ups and never let a stranger control your computer. Close the window, restart, and ask someone you know.
