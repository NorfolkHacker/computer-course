## The Operator says

Luser list, entry one: "screen is broken". The screen is never broken. The screen is a window. You were looking through a window at a house with the lights off.

Here is the thing nobody tells you. A computer is just a handful of parts, and each part has one job. Once you know which part does which job, half of all computer problems become "oh, *that* bit isn't switched on." And the other half become my problem, which, frankly, is what I'm here for. Reluctantly.

## The Mender's way

A computer is a few parts working together. Learn their names and their jobs; everything else in this course builds on them.

### The parts and their jobs

| Part | What it looks like | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| **The computer itself** | A box under or beside the desk (a "tower"), or the whole of a laptop | The thinking. This is the real computer; everything else plugs into it. |
| **Monitor** (or **screen**) | The flat thing that looks like a TV | Shows you pictures. It does no thinking. It has its own power button. |
| **Keyboard** | Rows of keys with letters and numbers | Typing words and giving commands. Lesson C. |
| **Mouse** (or **touchpad** on a laptop) | A small thing that fits your hand, or a flat patch below a laptop keyboard | Moving the pointer on the screen and clicking. Lesson B. |
| **Speakers / headphones** | Small boxes, or built into the screen or laptop | Sound. |
| **Webcam** | A tiny lens at the top of the screen | Video calls. Lesson V. |
| **Cables and sockets** | Wires; holes in the back and sides | Power, and connecting things together. Lesson U. |

A **laptop** is all of these in one folding case: the bottom half is the computer, keyboard and touchpad; the top half is the screen, webcam and speakers.

Inside the box are a few parts you'll hear about. You never need to open it, but the words come up:

- **Processor** (CPU): the brain that does the thinking.
- **Memory** (RAM): the computer's desk space; what it's working on *right now*. Empty when it's switched off.
- **Storage** (a drive, often called an SSD): the computer's filing cabinet, where your files are kept even when it's off.

### Switching on

1. Make sure the computer is plugged in at the wall, and the wall switch is on.
2. Find the **power button** on the computer itself. On a tower it is usually on the front or top, with this symbol: ⏻. On a laptop it is above the keyboard or on the side.
3. Press it **once**, briefly. Don't hold it down.
4. If the screen stays dark, press the **monitor's** power button too (usually on its bottom edge or underneath).
5. Wait. A logo appears, then a **lock screen** with the time on it. This can take up to a minute.
6. Click anywhere or press any key, then type your **PIN** or **password** and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. You'll see the **desktop**: the main screen, with the **taskbar** along the bottom.

### Switching off properly

Never switch off at the wall or hold the power button while the computer is working. That's like pulling a book away from someone mid-sentence: work can be lost.

1. Click the **Start** button: the Windows logo ⊞ in the taskbar at the bottom of the screen.
2. Click the **power** icon ⏻ in the bottom-right corner of the Start menu.
3. Click **Shut down**. Wait until the power light on the computer goes off.

The other choices in that menu:

- **Sleep**: a quick nap. The screen goes off and it uses very little power; press a key or the power button to wake it, and everything is where you left it.
- **Restart**: switches off and straight back on. This fixes a surprising number of problems (Lesson T).

### When something is stuck

Only if the computer has completely frozen (nothing moves for several minutes): hold the power button down for about 10 seconds until it switches off, wait a moment, then switch it on normally. This is the emergency exit, not the front door.

## Do it now

1. Point to each part of your own computer and say its name and job out loud: computer, monitor, keyboard, mouse, speakers, webcam.
2. Find the power button on the computer and on the monitor.
3. Shut the computer down properly using **Start** → power icon → **Shut down**.
4. Wait until the light goes off. Count to ten.
5. Switch it back on with **one** short press and sign in.

You've done it when you're looking at the desktop, and you got there without holding any button down.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: The screen is dark but the computer's power light is on. What should you check first?
  options:
    - The computer is broken; ring someone
    - The monitor's own power button
    - Hit it with the Whudonk Stick
  answer: 1
  why: The monitor has its own power button. If the computer is on but the screen is dark, the screen is usually just switched off.
- q: What is the right way to switch a computer off?
  options:
    - Hold the power button down until it goes off
    - Switch it off at the wall
    - Start, then the power icon, then Shut down
  answer: 2
  why: Shut down from the Start menu lets the computer save and close everything first. Holding the button is only for a computer that has completely frozen.
- q: Which part keeps your files safe even when the computer is switched off?
  options:
    - Storage (the drive)
    - Memory (RAM)
    - The monitor
  answer: 0
  why: Storage is the filing cabinet; it keeps files when the power is off. Memory is just the desk space for what's open right now.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble loves a computer that is "just left on" for weeks, never restarted, never updated. It hides in the gaps. Once a week, do a proper **Shut down** or **Restart**: it clears out the computer's memory and lets it finish installing updates, which is how holes get patched before the Scribble finds them. Lesson S has the rest.
