## The Operator says

Luser list, entry fourteen: "My phone won't leave me alone." Your phone is not haunted. You tapped **Allow** for every app that asked to send notifications.

**Settings** is the control room. Bigger text, quieter nights, fewer pings, a screen that doesn't blind you at midnight: it's all in there. Learn its search bar and you'll never need to remember where anything is.

## The Mender's way

**Settings** controls how your computer and phone behave. Both have a search bar that finds any setting by name.

### Finding Settings

### On Windows

1. Click **Start** (the Windows logo ⊞ on the taskbar), then **Settings** (a gear ⚙). Or press <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>I</kbd>.
2. The search box, **Find a setting**, is at the top-left. Type what you want (for example `text size`) and click the result.

### On Android

1. Swipe down twice from the top of the screen and tap the **gear** ⚙ (usually bottom-right of Quick Settings). Or open the **Settings** app from the app drawer.
2. Tap **Search settings** at the top. Type what you want and tap the result.
3. Menu names vary by maker. Steps below use Google Pixel wording; if yours differs, use the search.

### Bigger text and bigger everything

**Text size** makes only the words bigger; **display size** or **scale** makes everything bigger.

### On Windows

- **Text size:** **Settings** → **Accessibility** → **Text size**. Drag the slider to the right, then click **Apply**.
- **Everything bigger:** **Settings** → **System** → **Display** → **Scale**. Pick a bigger percentage, such as **125%** or **150%**.

### On Android

- **Settings** → **Accessibility** → **Display size and text**.
- Drag the **Font size** slider for bigger words, and the **Display size** slider for bigger everything.

### Brightness and night light

**Night light** makes the screen warmer and more orange in the evening, which is easier on the eyes.

### On Windows

1. Click the **network, volume and battery** icons together in the bottom-right corner of the taskbar (or press <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>A</kbd>). This is **Quick Settings**.
2. Drag the **brightness** slider (with a sun ☀). Desktop monitors may not show it; use the monitor's own buttons.
3. For night light: **Settings** → **System** → **Display** → **Night light**. Click **Turn on now**, or turn on **Schedule night light** to have it come on at sunset.

### On Android

1. Swipe down twice from the top. Drag the **brightness** slider.
2. For night light: **Settings** → **Display & touch** (on older phones just **Display**) → **Night Light** (on the newest Pixels it's inside **Comfort filters**), then **Schedule** → **Turns on from sunset to sunrise**. On Samsung phones it's called **Eye comfort shield**. If you can't find it, search Settings for `night light`.

### Sound and volume

### On Windows

- Click the **speaker** icon in the bottom-right of the taskbar and drag the volume slider.
- **Settings** → **System** → **Sound** lets you choose speakers or headphones.

### On Android

- Press a **volume** button on the side. A slider appears.
- Tap the **three dots** or small icon below the slider to see each volume separately: media, calls, ring and alarms.
- More: **Settings** → **Sound & vibration**.

### Quiet time: Do Not Disturb and Focus

**Do Not Disturb** silences notifications and calls for a while, without switching anything off. Alarms still go off.

### On Windows

1. **Settings** → **System** → **Notifications**.
2. Turn on **Do not disturb**. Below it, **Turn on do not disturb automatically** lets you choose times, such as every night.
3. **Focus** (**Settings** → **System** → **Focus**) turns on Do Not Disturb for a set time, say 30 minutes.

### On Android

1. Swipe down twice and tap the **Do Not Disturb** tile (on some phones the tile is called **Modes**). Or go to **Settings** → **Modes** → **Do Not Disturb** (on older phones: **Settings** → **Notifications** → **Do Not Disturb**).
2. Under **People**, you can let chosen contacts through.
3. To have it come on every night automatically, go to **Settings** → **Modes** → **Bedtime** and set your times. On older phones, tap **Schedules** inside Do Not Disturb. If you can't find either, search Settings for `do not disturb`.

### Turning off one app's notifications

### On Windows

1. **Settings** → **System** → **Notifications**.
2. Scroll down to **Notifications from apps and other senders**.
3. Turn off the switch next to the app that's annoying you.

### On Android

1. When the notification next appears, swipe down and **press and hold** it.
2. Tap **Turn off notifications** (or the gear ⚙ to choose which kinds to keep).
3. Or: **Settings** → **Notifications** → **App notifications**, and turn off the switch next to each app you don't want to hear from.

When a new app asks to send you notifications, only tap **Allow** if you really want to hear from it.

## Do it now

1. On the phone, find one app whose notifications you never read (a game or a shop) and turn its notifications off.
2. Set up Do Not Disturb on the phone to come on automatically at night.
3. On the computer, open **Settings** and use **Find a setting** to search for `night light`. Turn on **Schedule night light**.

You've done it when the phone stays quiet tonight and you found night light by searching.

## Scribble watch

The Scribble loves the **Allow notifications?** button on websites. Say yes, and a dodgy site can send fake "virus detected!" alerts that look like they come from your computer, even after you've left the site. When a website asks to show notifications, click **Block**. If they're already arriving, search Settings in your browser for `notifications` and remove that site.
