## The Operator says

Rule three. There is a box at the top of the browser for addresses. There is a box in the middle of the page for searches. There is a box at the bottom of the screen for Windows. Lusers use them at random, like a fruit machine, then ring me when they win a virus.

Half of life lives online now: the bank, the doctor, the council. Get connected, then go exactly where you mean to go, not "close enough". Address bar, tabs, back, favourites. That's the toolkit.

## The Mender's way

First, some words:

- The **internet** joins computers all over the world.
- **Wi-Fi** is how your computer or phone connects to it without a cable, through the **router** (the box with lights from your internet company).
- A **website** is a collection of pages, like the BBC's.
- A **web address** is a website's exact name, such as `bbc.co.uk`. It never has spaces.
- A **browser** is the app you use to look at websites. On Windows it's usually **Microsoft Edge** (a blue-green wave icon); on Android it's usually **Chrome** (a red, yellow and green circle).

### Connecting to Wi-Fi

You need your Wi-Fi's **name** and **password**. These are usually printed on a sticker on the router, often labelled **Wireless network name** (or **SSID**) and **Wi-Fi password** (or **Network key**).

### On Windows

1. Click the Wi-Fi icon at the bottom right of the taskbar, near the clock (a fan of curved lines).
2. A panel opens. Make sure the **Wi-Fi** button is switched on (coloured in).
3. Click the small **>** arrow on the right side of the **Wi-Fi** button to see a list of networks.
4. Click your network's name. Tick **Connect automatically**, then click **Connect**.
5. Type the password from the router exactly, and click **Next**.
6. When it says **Connected, secured**, you're online.

### On Android

Menus vary by maker. On Google/Pixel phones:

1. Open **Settings** (the gear icon).
2. Tap **Network & internet**, then **Internet**. (On Samsung: **Connections**, then **Wi-Fi**.) If you can't find it, type `wifi` into the search bar at the top of **Settings**.
3. Make sure **Wi-Fi** is switched on.
4. Tap your network's name, type the password, and tap **Connect**.
5. When it says **Connected**, you're online.

After that, they reconnect by themselves.

### Going to a website: the address bar

The **address bar** is the long box across the **very top** of the browser window. It shows the address of the page you're on. To go somewhere:

1. Open **Edge** (or **Chrome** on your phone).
2. Click once in the address bar.
3. Type the web address, such as `bbc.co.uk`, and press <kbd>Enter</kbd> (on the phone, tap the **Go** or arrow key).

The address bar goes straight to the website. Any other box (the Windows search box, or a search box in a web page) gives you search results instead, often with adverts at the top.

### Getting around

| Thing | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Back** ← | Top left, beside the address bar | Goes back to the page you were on before. |
| **Refresh** ⟳ | Next to Back | Loads the page again if it looks stuck. |
| **Tabs** | Above the address bar | Each tab is a separate page in the same window. Click a tab to switch to it. |
| **New tab** + | To the right of the last tab | Opens a fresh tab. Or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd>. |
| **Close tab** ✕ | On the right of each tab | Closes just that page. Or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>W</kbd>. |

On Android, use the back gesture or button to go back. In Chrome, the square with a number (top right) shows your tabs.

### Saving a website for later

Edge calls saved websites **favourites**; Chrome calls them **bookmarks**.

- **Edge**: on the page you want, click the star ☆ at the right-hand end of the address bar, then **Done**. (Or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>.) To open one later, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>O</kbd> (or click the **Favourites** button on the toolbar).
- **Chrome on Android**: tap ⋮ (top right), then the star ☆ at the top of the menu. To open one later, tap ⋮, then **Bookmarks**.

## Do it now

1. Check the computer and phone are on Wi-Fi.
2. Open **Edge**. Click in the address bar at the top, type `bbc.co.uk` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
3. Click a headline, then **Back** ←.
4. Open a new tab with **+**. In its address bar, type `metoffice.gov.uk` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
5. Add the Met Office page to your favourites with the star ☆.
6. Close that tab with its ✕. Open **Favourites** and click the Met Office to bring it back.

You've done it when you've typed two addresses into the address bar and reopened one from your favourites.

## Quiz

```quiz
    - The search box in the middle of a web page
  answer: 1
  why: The address bar goes straight to the address you type. The other boxes give you search results, often with adverts first.

  options:
    - The app you use to look at websites, such as Edge or Chrome
    - The box with lights that gives you Wi-Fi
    - A website for buying things
  answer: 0
  why: Edge and Chrome are browsers. The router is the box with lights; websites are what the browser shows you.
- q: You clicked a link and want the previous page again. What do you do?
  options:
    - Close the browser and start again
    - Type the old address from memory
    - Click the Back arrow at the top left
  answer: 2
  why: Back returns you to the page you were just on. On a phone, use the back gesture or back button.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble loves the little box that says *"This site wants to show notifications"*. Click **Allow** on the wrong site and it can send fake virus warnings to the corner of your screen for weeks. Habit: on any website you don't know well, click **Block** (or **Don't allow**) whenever a site asks to send notifications. Real websites work perfectly well without it.
