## Cold open

*Psych and Otic at the desk. On the screen, a small yellow folder icon.*

**Otic:** The Mender says double-click it to open it.

**Psych:** Not a problem... *He clicks once. Has a sip of tea. Clicks again.*

*Nothing happens. The folder just turns blue.*

**Psych:** Double. Click. *Click. A long pause. Click.*

*Still nothing. Psych's hand wanders as he clicks. The folder slides off across the screen and lands somewhere else entirely.*

**Otic:** It's running away!

**Otic's cow:** MOOOOO!

*Psych lifts the mouse off the desk and waves it at the screen like a TV remote. The pointer doesn't move.*

*The Whudonk Stick begins to glow.*

## The Operator says

Luser list, entry two: "double-click doesn't work". It works. It just doesn't work at the speed of a man making tea between clicks.

The mouse is how you point at things and tell the computer "that one". Every lesson after this one says "click here" or "right-click there", so this is the bit you practise until your hand does it without asking you. Ten minutes now saves a lifetime of folders wandering off. I still have dreams.

## The Mender's way

The mouse moves a small arrow on the screen called the **pointer**. Whatever the pointer is resting on is what your clicks will affect. Rest your hand on the mouse, keep it flat on the desk, and slide it. Don't lift it; the mouse only works while it touches the desk.

If you run out of desk, lift the mouse, put it back down in the middle, and carry on sliding. The pointer stays where it was while the mouse is in the air.

### The buttons

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Left button** | Under your first finger | The main button. "Click" always means this one. |
| **Right button** | Under your middle finger | Opens a menu of choices for whatever you're pointing at. |
| **Scroll wheel** | The little wheel between them | Moves a page up and down. |

### The six moves

1. **Point**: slide the mouse so the tip of the arrow sits on the thing you want.
2. **Click**: press the left button once and let go. This *selects* something (it goes blue) or presses an on-screen button.
3. **Double-click**: press the left button twice, quickly, without moving the mouse in between. This *opens* things on the desktop and in folders, such as files, folders and apps. If it doesn't work, try again faster, keeping your hand still.
4. **Right-click**: press the right button once. A small menu appears next to the pointer with things you can do to that item. Click a choice to use it, or press <kbd>Esc</kbd> (top-left of the keyboard) to close the menu without doing anything.
5. **Drag**: point at something, press the left button and *hold it down*, slide the mouse to move the thing, then let go of the button to drop it. This is how you move icons, files and windows.
6. **Scroll**: roll the wheel towards you to move down a page, away from you to move up.

A simple rule: **one click to choose, two clicks to open**. Links on websites and buttons in apps only ever need one click.

### On a laptop: the touchpad

A laptop usually has a **touchpad**: a flat smooth patch below the keyboard that does the mouse's job.

- **Point**: slide one finger across the touchpad.
- **Click**: tap once with one finger, or press down on the bottom-left of the pad until it clicks.
- **Double-click**: tap twice quickly.
- **Right-click**: tap with two fingers together, or press down on the bottom-right of the pad.
- **Drag**: tap twice, keep your finger down on the second tap, then slide.
- **Scroll**: put two fingers on the pad and slide them up or down together.

If the pointer is too fast or too slow, change it: open **Start**, then **Settings**, then **Bluetooth & devices**, then **Mouse** (or **Touchpad**), and move the **Mouse pointer speed** slider. If you can't find it, type "mouse" into the search box at the top of **Settings**.

### On Android (your phone)

Your phone has no pointer: your finger *is* the pointer. The same moves have touch versions:

| Mouse | Phone |
|---|---|
| Click | **Tap**: touch the screen briefly and lift. |
| Double-click | Not needed: one tap opens things. A **double-tap** zooms photos and maps. |
| Right-click | **Long-press**: touch and hold for about a second until a menu pops up. |
| Drag | Long-press, then slide your finger without lifting it, then let go. |
| Scroll | **Swipe**: slide your finger up or down the screen. |

## Do it now

1. On the desktop, find the **Recycle Bin** icon (top-left, usually a little bin).
2. Point at it. Click it once: it goes blue. Click an empty part of the desktop: the blue goes away.
3. Double-click the **Recycle Bin**. A window opens. Close it with the **X** in its top-right corner (one click).
4. Right-click an empty part of the desktop. A menu appears. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to close it.
5. Drag the **Recycle Bin** icon a little way down the screen, then drag it back.
6. Open **Start**, click **Settings**, scroll down and up with the wheel, then close it with the **X**.

You've done it when you can open the Recycle Bin with one double-click on the first try, three times in a row.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: You double-click a folder and nothing opens; it just goes blue. What's the most likely reason?
  options:
    - The folder is broken
    - The two clicks were too slow, or the mouse moved between them
    - You need to lift the mouse and point it at the screen
  answer: 1
  why: A double-click has to be two quick clicks with the mouse kept still. A slow double-click counts as two single clicks, which only selects the folder.
- q: How do you see a menu of choices for something on the screen?
  options:
    - Double-click it
    - Drag it
    - Right-click it
    - Scroll over it
  answer: 2
  why: The right button opens a menu of things you can do to whatever you're pointing at. Press Esc to close it if you change your mind.
- q: On your Android phone, what does the same job as a right-click?
  options:
    - A long-press (touch and hold)
    - A double-tap
    - Shaking the phone
  answer: 0
  why: Touching and holding for about a second brings up a menu of choices, just like a right-click on the computer.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble loves an itchy clicking finger. It puts big shiny **DOWNLOAD** and **CLICK HERE** buttons on web pages and in pop-ups, hoping you'll click before you read. Habit: **point first, read, then click**. Rest the pointer on a button and read what it says before you press. If you're not sure what something will do, don't click it; close the window with its **X** instead.
