## The Operator says

Every update you postpone is a door you've left unlocked, with a little sign on it saying "Back in a month". The burglars can read. Updates aren't Microsoft fussing; they're the patches for holes that the bad people already know about.

And for the record: Windows has its own antivirus, built in and switched on. It's good. It's free. Anyone selling you a "cleaner" or a "booster" is selling you a box of fog. I have a whole drawer of excuses for broken computers. "Never restarted it" is the only one that's always true.

## The Mender's way

Keeping safe is mostly letting the protection you already have do its job: updates, the built-in antivirus, and a lock on the screen.

### On Windows: Windows Update

An **update** is a fix or improvement sent out by Microsoft. Many of them close **security holes**: weaknesses that criminals could use to get in.

1. Click **Start**, then **Settings**.
2. Click **Windows Update** (at the bottom of the list on the left).
3. Click **Check for updates**. Windows downloads anything new by itself.
4. If it says **Restart required** or shows a **Restart now** button, save your work, close your documents, and click **Restart now**.
5. Wait. The computer may say **Working on updates** and restart more than once. Don't switch it off while it does.

Updates are only finished after the restart. That's why restarting once a week matters. You can see past updates under **Update history** on the same page.

### On Windows: Windows Security

**Antivirus** is software that looks for harmful programs (**malware**, such as viruses) and removes them. Windows 11 has one built in called **Microsoft Defender**, and you manage it with the **Windows Security** app. It runs all the time and updates itself. For home use it is enough; you don't need to buy another.

To check on it:

1. Click **Start**, type `Windows Security`, and open it.
2. Look at the **Security at a glance** page. Green ticks mean all is well.
3. A yellow or red mark means something needs attention. Click it and follow the button it offers (often **Turn on** or **Restart now**).
4. To run a check yourself, click **Virus & threat protection**, then **Quick scan**.

You'll also see **Firewall & network protection**. A **firewall** is a guard on your internet connection that blocks unwanted traffic coming in. It's on by default. Leave it on.

**Don't pay for cleaners, boosters or "PC optimisers".** They find scary-sounding "problems" that aren't real, and some are malware themselves.

### On Android: system updates

Android phones get two kinds of updates. Menu names vary by maker; these are the Google Pixel names. On any phone, you can open **Settings** and type `update` into the search bar at the top.

**Android system update** (the big one):

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **System**, then **Software updates**, then **System update**. (On Samsung: **Software update**, then **Download and install**.)
3. If an update is available, tap **Download and install**. When asked, tap **Restart now**.

**Google Play system update** (smaller security fixes from Google):

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Security & privacy**, then **System & updates**, then **Google Play system update**.
3. If one is waiting, install it and restart.

App updates come from the **Play Store**: tap your profile picture (top right), then **Manage apps & device**, then **Update all**.

### On Android: Play Protect

**Google Play Protect** is the phone's built-in malware checker. It scans your apps automatically.

1. Open **Play Store**.
2. Tap your profile picture (top right), then **Play Protect**.
3. It should say **No harmful apps found**. You can tap **Scan** to check now.

Keep it switched on (the **⚙** gear icon on that page shows the setting).

### Locking your screen

A **screen lock** means nobody can use your device without your PIN, password, fingerprint or face.

### On Windows (locking the PC)

Press <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>L</kbd> every time you walk away. Sign in with your PIN to come back.

### On Android (locking the phone)

1. Open **Settings**, then **Security & privacy**, then **Device unlock**, then **Screen lock**. (Or search Settings for `screen lock`.)
2. Choose **PIN** (at least six numbers, not your birthday) and follow the steps.
3. You can add a **fingerprint** or **face** unlock on the same page.

Press the power button once to lock the phone straight away.

## Do it now

1. On your PC, open **Settings**, then **Windows Update**, and click **Check for updates**. Install anything it finds and restart if asked.
2. Open **Windows Security** and check for green ticks.
3. On your phone, open **Settings**, search for `update`, and check for a system update.
4. Make sure your phone has a screen lock.

You've done it when Windows Update says **You're up to date**, Windows Security shows green ticks, and your phone asks for a PIN when you wake it.
