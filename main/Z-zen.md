This last lesson is the boring one that makes the others stick. A computer, like a shed, gets cluttered whether you use it or not. Ten minutes a month keeps it fast and safe. And when something does go wrong, the way you ask for help decides whether you get a fix in five minutes or a shrug. Then there's an exam. I wrote it myself. You'll be fine. Probably.

## The Mender's way

Keep things tidy a little and often, ask for help clearly, and keep learning. That's all.

### The monthly 10-minute routine

Pick a day each month and do these five things.

### Step 1: Updates

### On Windows

1. **Start** → **Settings** → **Windows Update**.
2. Click **Check for updates**, then **Download & install** if anything is waiting.

### On Android

1. **Settings** → **System** → **Software updates** (Samsung: **Software update**).
2. Tap to check, and install anything offered. Also open the **Play Store**, tap your **profile picture**, then **Manage apps & device**, then **Update all**.

### Step 2: Restart

On the computer: **Start** → power icon → **Restart**. On the phone: hold the power button (with volume up on some phones) and tap **Restart**.

### Step 3: Empty Downloads

1. Open **File Explorer** and click **Downloads** on the left.
2. Move anything you want to keep into **Documents** or **Pictures** (Lesson E).
3. Select the rest (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>A</kbd> selects everything) and press <kbd>Delete</kbd>.
4. On the desktop, right-click the **Recycle Bin** and click **Empty Recycle Bin**.

### Step 4: Check backups (Lesson L)

- On the computer, click the **OneDrive** cloud icon at the bottom-right of the taskbar (click the ^ arrow if you can't see it). It should say your files are up to date.
- On the phone, open **Google Photos** and tap your **profile picture**: it should say backup is complete.

### Step 5: Clear out apps you don't use

- Windows: **Start** → **Settings** → **Apps** → **Installed apps**. Click the **...** next to an app you don't use, then **Uninstall**.
- Android: press and hold the app's icon, then tap **Uninstall**.

Only remove apps you recognise. If in doubt, leave it.

### Asking for help well

Who to ask: a family member or friend you trust, the company that made the thing (using contact details from its real website), or a library digital help session. Never a stranger who rang you.

What to tell them:

1. **What you were trying to do.** "I was trying to print a letter from Word."
2. **What happened instead**, including the **exact message**. Better still, send a **screenshot**: a picture of the screen. On Windows press <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd> and drag a box; on Android press **Power** and **Volume down** together.
3. **What you've already tried.** "I restarted it. Same thing."

Never give a helper your passwords or codes, and never let anyone you don't know control your computer remotely.

### Keep learning

Nobody knows everything about computers. What you have now is the habit: read the message, search for the answer (Lesson H), try it safely, and ask well when stuck. Come back to any lesson whenever you need it.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: Windows says "Restart required". What's the right habit?
  options:
    - Click Remind me later, every day, forever
    - Save your work and restart soon, so updates can finish
    - Hold the power button down until it goes off
  answer: 1
  why: Many updates only finish installing on a restart. Save your work and use Start, power, Restart.
- q: Which message to a helper will get you the quickest fix?
  options:
    - "Computer broken pls fix"
    - "It's doing the thing again"
    - "Trying to print from Word, it says 'Printer offline', I've restarted both, here's a screenshot"
  answer: 2
  why: Say what you were doing, the exact message, and what you've tried. A screenshot saves a lot of guessing.
- q: Final question. A stranger rings, says your computer has a virus, and asks to take control of it to fix it. What do you do?
  options:
    - Hang up. You know this one.
    - Let them in; they sound like they know computers
    - Give them your password but not remote control
  answer: 0
  why: Genuine companies don't ring you about viruses. Hang up, and if you're worried, ask someone you trust using a number you already have.
```
