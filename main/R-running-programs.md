## The Operator says

Luser list, entry twenty-two: "I only installed one thing". You installed nine. Eight of them send me postcards from your bank account.

Getting new programs is easy. Getting *only* the program you wanted is the skill. Get apps from the official shop or the maker's own website, and read the box before you click **Yes**. That habit keeps out most of the rubbish I'd otherwise scrape off your computer with a spatula.

## The Mender's way

A **program** (or **app**, short for application) is something that runs on your computer or phone: a browser, a game, a photo editor. **Installing** means putting a new app on. **Uninstalling** means taking it off properly.

### Where to get apps, in order

1. **The official app shop:** the **Microsoft Store** on Windows, the **Google Play Store** on Android. Apps there are checked and update themselves.
2. **The company's own website.** If the app isn't in the shop, go to the maker's real website (for example, **zoom.us** for Zoom). Type the address yourself, or check the address bar carefully (Lesson H).
3. **Never** from "free download" websites, adverts, or pop-ups saying you need something. Big green **DOWNLOAD** buttons are usually adverts that install junk.

### On Windows: the Microsoft Store

1. Click **Start**, type `Microsoft Store`, and open it.
2. Click the search box at the top and type the app's name.
3. Click the right app. Check the name of the maker underneath the title.
4. Click **Get** (or **Install**). If it says a price, it costs money.
5. When it's done, the button changes to **Open**. The app is also in the **Start** menu.

### On Windows: from a website

Sometimes you need an app that isn't in the Store.

1. Go to the maker's own website and find its **Download** link.
2. The browser downloads a file, often ending in `.exe` or `.msi`. This is an **installer**: a small program whose only job is to put the real app on your computer.
3. Open it from the browser's downloads list (the arrow at the top-right of the browser) or from your **Downloads** folder.
4. Windows will ask a question (see below). Then follow the installer's steps, reading each page. Untick any box offering extra programs or "special offers".
5. When it's finished, you can delete the installer file from **Downloads**; the app stays.

### "Do you want to allow this app to make changes to your device?"

This box is **User Account Control** (UAC). The screen dims and Windows asks permission before anything changes its deep parts. It shows the app's name and the **Verified publisher** (the company that made it).

- Say **Yes** only if **you** just started something on purpose, such as an installer you chose, *and* the name and publisher match what you expected.
- Say **No** if it appeared out of nowhere, if you didn't just click something, or if the publisher says **Unknown** and you're not sure. Saying **No** is always safe; nothing is changed.

### On Windows: uninstalling an app

1. Click **Start**, then **Settings**.
2. Click **Apps** on the left, then **Installed apps**.
3. Find the app in the list (there's a search box at the top).
4. Click the **⋯** (three dots) on the right of its row, then **Uninstall**.
5. Click **Uninstall** again to confirm. If a UAC box appears, click **Yes**, because you started this.

### On Android: the Play Store

**Installing:**

1. Open **Play Store**.
2. Tap the **Search** box and type the app's name.
3. Tap the right app. Check the maker's name under the title.
4. Tap **Install**. When it's done, tap **Open**, or find it in your app list.

**Uninstalling:**

1. Long-press (touch and hold) the app's icon.
2. Tap **App info** (sometimes an **ⓘ** icon).
3. Tap **Uninstall**, then **OK**.

### App permissions on Android

A **permission** is something an app asks to use, such as your camera, microphone, location or contacts.

- **Allow** it if it makes sense. A video-calling app needs the camera.
- **Don't allow** if it doesn't. A torch app has no reason to read your contacts.
- If offered, choose **While using the app** or **Only this time** rather than all the time.

To check later: long-press the app icon, tap **App info**, then **Permissions**. Tap any permission to change it. To see every app's permissions at once, open **Settings** and search for `Permission manager`.

## Do it now

1. On your PC, open **Settings**, then **Apps**, then **Installed apps**. Scroll through, just looking. Anything called a "cleaner" or "booster" is worth asking someone about.
2. On your phone, open **Play Store** and install a small, useful free app from a well-known maker, such as **Google Keep**.
3. Open it once, then uninstall it using **App info**.

You've done it when you've installed and removed an app on your phone, and you know where the list of installed apps on your PC lives.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: Where should you look first for a new app on Windows?
  options:
    - The Microsoft Store
    - The first "free download" site in a web search
    - A pop-up advert offering it
  answer: 0
  why: Apps in the Microsoft Store are checked and install cleanly. Download sites and adverts often bundle in junk or worse.
- q: The screen dims and asks "Do you want to allow this app to make changes to your device?" but you didn't just open anything. What do you do?
  options:
    - Click Yes so it goes away
    - Click No
    - Click Yes several times to be sure
  answer: 1
  why: Only say Yes when you started something on purpose and recognise the name. If it appeared by itself, No is the safe answer and changes nothing.
- q: How do you remove an app properly on Windows?
  options:
    - Delete its icon from the desktop
    - Settings, Apps, Installed apps, then the three dots and Uninstall
    - Drag it into the Recycle Bin
  answer: 1
  why: Deleting a desktop icon only removes a shortcut. Uninstalling from Installed apps removes the program itself.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble's favourite trick is a pop-up shouting "Your PC is slow! Install our FREE cleaner!" The cleaner is the Scribble. Close the browser tab with the cross. If it won't close, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Esc</kbd> and end the browser (Lesson T). Never click anything inside the pop-up.
