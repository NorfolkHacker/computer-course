## The Operator says

Luser list, entry nine: "I only meant to send it to one person." There is no button that un-tells forty-three relatives what you think of the burgers.

Email is still how banks, doctors, the council and every website you sign up to talk to you. Learn the handful of buttons, read the **To** line before you press **Send**, and you'll stay off my list.

## The Mender's way

Email is a letter that arrives on your screen instead of your doormat. Here are the parts, then how to read, write and reply.

### What an email address is

An **email address** is like a postal address for messages. It always has an **@** sign (said "at") in the middle, for example `psych.otic@gmail.com`.

- Before the **@**: the person's mailbox name.
- After the **@**: the company that looks after it (Gmail, Outlook and so on).
- There are no spaces. Capital letters don't matter.

Your **inbox** is the list of emails you've received. The newest is at the top. Emails you haven't opened yet are shown in **bold**.

This lesson uses **Gmail** (Google's email) and **Outlook** (Microsoft's). The buttons are almost the same in both.

### The parts of an email

| Box | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **To** | The address of the person the email is for. |
| **Cc** | "Copy": people who should see it but aren't the main person. Everyone can see who was copied. |
| **Subject** | A short title, like "Barbecue on Saturday". It's what people see in their inbox. |
| The big box | Your message. |

### Opening your email

### On Windows (Gmail or Outlook in the browser)

1. Open your web browser (Lesson G).
2. For Gmail, go to `gmail.com`. For Outlook, go to `outlook.com`.
3. Sign in with your email address and password.
4. Your inbox appears. Click once on an email to read it; click **Inbox** on the left to go back.

### On Android (the Gmail app)

1. Tap the **Gmail** app (a red and multicoloured **M**). It's already on most Android phones.
2. Your inbox appears. Tap an email to read it.

### Writing a new email

1. In Gmail on the computer, click **Compose** (top-left). In the Gmail app, tap **Compose** (bottom-right, with a pencil). In Outlook, click **New mail** (top-left).
2. In **To**, type the person's full email address.
3. In **Subject**, type a short title.
4. Click or tap in the big box and type your message.
5. Read the **To** line once more.
6. Click or tap **Send** (in the app, it's a paper-aeroplane icon at the top).

### Reply and Reply all

At the bottom of an open email (or in the top-right of it) you'll find:

- **Reply**: answers **only the person who sent it**. Use this almost always.
- **Reply all**: answers **the sender and everyone else on the To and Cc lines**. Use it only when everyone really needs your answer.
- **Forward**: sends a copy of the email on to someone new, whose address you type in.

Before you press **Send** on any reply, look at the **To** line and check who is on it.

### Attachments

An **attachment** is a file (a photo, a letter, a PDF) sent along with an email. It shows as a little box or icon at the bottom of the message, sometimes with a paperclip 📎.

To **open** an attachment:

1. Click or tap the attachment box at the bottom of the email.
2. It opens in a viewer. To keep it, click the **download** icon (a down arrow); it goes into **Downloads** (Lesson F).

To **send** an attachment:

1. Start a new email or a reply.
2. Click the **paperclip** icon 📎. In Gmail on the computer it's along the bottom of the message box; in the Gmail app it's at the top. In Outlook it's labelled **Attach file**.
3. Find the file and click or tap it, then **Open** (on the computer).
4. Wait until the file's name appears in the email, then **Send**.

### The spam folder

**Spam** (or **junk**) is unwanted email: adverts and scams. Gmail and Outlook move most of it into a separate folder.

- **Gmail on the computer:** in the list on the left, click **More**, then **Spam**.
- **Gmail app:** tap the three lines ☰ (top-left), then **Spam**.
- **Outlook:** click **Junk Email** in the list on the left.

If an email you're expecting hasn't arrived, look in the spam folder. If it's there, open it and click **Not spam** (Gmail) or **Not junk** (Outlook).

## Do it now

1. Open Gmail or Outlook on the computer.
2. Write a new email **to your own email address**. Subject: `Test`. Message: `Hello me`.
3. Attach one photo or document using the paperclip.
4. Check the **To** line, then **Send**.
5. On your phone, open the Gmail app (or Outlook) and find the email in your inbox.
6. Tap the attachment to open it.
7. Tap **Reply**, type `Got it`, and send.

You've done it when the reply arrives back in your inbox on the computer, and you've checked the spam folder at least once.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: An email went to the whole family and you want to answer only the person who sent it. Which button?
  options:
    - Reply all
    - Reply
    - Forward
  answer: 1
  why: Reply goes only to the sender. Reply all goes to everyone on the To and Cc lines.
- q: A friend says they emailed you, but it isn't in your inbox. Where should you look first?
  options:
    - The spam (or Junk Email) folder
    - The Recycle Bin on the desktop
    - Nowhere; it's lost
  answer: 0
  why: Real emails sometimes get mistaken for junk. Check the spam folder and mark it as Not spam if you find it.
- q: What does the Cc line do?
  options:
    - Hides the email from everyone else
    - Sends a copy to extra people, and everyone can see who was copied
    - Makes the email arrive faster
  answer: 1
  why: Cc means copy. The people on it get the email too, and their addresses are visible to everyone else on it.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble writes email too. It sends attachments like `Invoice_overdue.zip` from strangers, or from a friend's stolen account. If you weren't expecting the attachment, don't open it. Ask the person, by phone or a fresh message (not by replying), whether they really sent it.
