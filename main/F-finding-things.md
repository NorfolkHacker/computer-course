## What went wrong

*Otic's email is open. It has a PDF attached: a form from the council.*

**Otic:** It says download the form, fill it in, send it back.

*Psych clicks the attachment. Something flickers at the top of the screen and disappears.*

**Psych:** Downloaded. *He looks at the desktop. No form.* Hm.

*He clicks it again. Flicker. Again. Flicker. Again.*

**Otic:** Where's it going?

**Psych:** Not a problem... It's in the computer. Somewhere. *He opens File Explorer and starts double-clicking every folder he can see, at random.*

*Twenty minutes later. Somewhere inside a folder called "Downloads", unseen, sit "form.pdf", "form (1).pdf", "form (2).pdf" and "form (3).pdf".*

**Otic's cow:** MOOOOO!

## The Operator says

Excuse calendar, Tuesday: "Your file has been eaten by the cloud." It hasn't. It's in Downloads. It's always in Downloads. I could have that engraved on something and save myself a lot of phone calls.

Nobody remembers where they saved everything. The trick isn't a better memory; it's knowing the three places to look and letting the computer do the searching. Downloaded things go to one folder. Recent things show up in one list. And the search box will find almost anything if you give it one good word. That's this lesson. It's short. So are my phone calls, after this.

## The Mender's way

There are three good ways to find something: look in **Downloads**, look in **Recent**, or **search**. Try them in that order.

### Downloads: where the internet puts things

When you download something from a website or save an email attachment, it almost always goes into the **Downloads** folder.

1. Open **File Explorer** (the yellow folder on the taskbar, or <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>E</kbd>).
2. Click **Downloads** in the left-hand column.
3. Look at the top of the list: the newest things are usually shown first, grouped under headings like **Today** and **Yesterday**.

In your web browser, the downloads list also opens with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>J</kbd>. Click a file in that list to open it.

### Sorting by date

If the newest files aren't at the top:

1. In File Explorer, click **Sort** in the bar along the top of the window.
2. Click **Date modified**.
3. Click **Sort** again and make sure **Descending** is ticked. "Descending" here means newest first.

To see dates beside each file, click **View**, then **Details**.

### Recent: what you used lately

1. Open **File Explorer**. It opens on **Home**.
2. Under the heading **Recent** is a list of files you've opened lately, newest first. Double-click one to open it.

The **Start** menu also has a **Recommended** section with recent files and newly installed apps.

### Searching the whole computer

1. Click the search box on the taskbar (it says **Search**, next to the **Start** button), or press the <kbd>Windows</kbd> key and just start typing.
2. Type **one or two words** from the file's name, such as `council form`.
3. The top result is under **Best match**. Click it to open it.
4. To show only files, click **Documents** (or **Photos**, **Folders**) along the top of the results.

The same box finds apps (`calculator`) and settings (`mouse`). It may also show web results from **Bing**; those open in your browser and are not on your computer.

### Searching inside a folder

1. In **File Explorer**, open the folder you think the file is in (or click **This PC** to search everything).
2. Click the search box at the **top right** of the window. It says **Search** and the folder's name.
3. Type a word from the file's name and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Matching files appear in the window.
4. Click the ← arrow at the top left to clear the search and go back.

### On Android (your phone)

Menus vary by maker; these are the Google/Pixel names. On Samsung, use **My Files** instead of **Files**.

1. **Downloads**: open **Files**, tap **Browse**, then **Downloads**. Newest is first.
2. **Searching files**: in **Files**, tap the search bar at the top and type a word from the name.
3. **Photos**: open **Google Photos** (or **Gallery** on Samsung) and tap **Search**. You can type things like `cat` or `beach` and it finds matching pictures.
4. **Apps and settings**: on a Pixel, tap the search bar at the bottom of the home screen. Inside **Settings**, use the search bar at the top: type `wifi` or `brightness` and tap the result.

Phone downloads can also be found in your browser: in **Chrome**, tap ⋮ (top right), then **Downloads**.

## Do it now

1. Open **File Explorer** and click **Downloads**. Use **Sort** → **Date modified** so the newest file is at the top. (If it's empty, that's fine; that's tidy.)
2. Switch to **View** → **Details** and read the date beside the top file.
3. Click **Home** and look at the **Recent** list. Double-click something you recognise; close it again.
4. Click the taskbar search box and type `notepad`. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to open it. Close it.
5. In **File Explorer**, click **Documents**, then search for `Practice` (the folder from Lesson E). If you didn't do Lesson E, search for any word you know is in a file name.

You've done it when you've found the same thing two different ways: once by looking, once by searching.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: You clicked "Download" on a form and nothing seemed to happen. Where is it most likely to be?
  options:
    - On the desktop
    - In the Downloads folder
    - Nowhere; click Download again until it appears
  answer: 1
  why: Downloaded files go to Downloads. Clicking again just makes extra copies, named things like form (1).pdf.
- q: How do you get the newest files to the top of a folder in File Explorer?
  options:
    - Sort, then Date modified, with Descending ticked
    - Sort, then Name
    - Drag the newest files to the top by hand
  answer: 0
  why: Sorting by Date modified, descending, puts the most recently changed files first.
- q: What's the best thing to type into the search box to find a letter called "Gas bill complaint"?
  options:
    - Every folder name you can think of
    - The whole sentence of what you remember writing
    - A word or two from its name, like gas bill
  answer: 2
  why: Search matches words in the file's name. One or two good words is usually enough.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble loves a messy Downloads folder: among twenty forms, nobody notices one extra file they never asked for. Habit: when something new appears in **Downloads**, check you know where it came from before you open it. If you don't recognise it, or its name ends in `.exe` and you weren't installing anything, don't open it. Delete it.
