## The Operator says

Luser list, entry 3,001: "but it had a padlock". So does my stationery cupboard. Doesn't mean the stapler's honest.

Money is where the Scribble works hardest, because money is what it wants. Shopping and banking online are perfectly safe when you know four or five rules, and the rules are short. Break them and the money can be gone in seconds, with nobody to ring. Follow them and the worst that usually happens is a slow parcel. I've seen both. I prefer slow parcels.

## The Mender's way

Online shopping and banking are safe when you check where you are, pay the right way, and lock the account properly.

### Check you're on the real website

1. Look at the **address bar** at the top of the browser: the web address of the page you're on.
2. Read the main name, just before the `.co.uk` or `.com` and the first single `/`. In `https://www.argos.co.uk/basket`, the main name is **argos.co.uk**. In `https://argos.co.uk.deals-cheap.shop/`, the real site is **deals-cheap.shop**, which is not Argos.
3. Check the address starts with `https://`. In **Edge** you'll see a small **padlock** next to it; in **Chrome** it's a small icon like two sliders.

The padlock only means the connection is private. It does **not** mean the shop is honest. Scam sites have padlocks too. The address is what matters.

Safest of all: type the shop's name into the address bar yourself, or use a bookmark, rather than clicking an advert or a link in a message.

### Use your bank's official app

1. On your phone, open the **Play Store**.
2. Search for your bank's name.
3. Check the name shown under the app is your bank itself (for example **Barclays Bank UK PLC**), and it has many downloads and reviews.
4. Install it and sign in with the details your bank gave you.

Only ever install your bank's app from the **Play Store**, never from a link in a text or email.

### Too good to be true

Signs a deal is a scam:

- The price is far lower than everywhere else.
- "Only 3 left!", "Offer ends in 10 minutes!": pressure to hurry.
- You've never heard of the shop, and it only appeared on social media.
- It asks you to pay by **bank transfer**, gift cards or crypto.

If in doubt, search the shop's name plus the word *reviews* (Lesson H) and look at what other people say.

### Pay the protected way

| How you pay | Protection |
|---|---|
| **Credit card** | Best. For things costing between £100 and £30,000, the card company shares responsibility if things go wrong (called **Section 75**). |
| **PayPal** | Good. **PayPal Buyer Protection** can refund you if goods don't arrive or aren't as described. |
| **Debit card** | Some protection: your bank can try to reclaim it (called **chargeback**). |
| **Bank transfer** | Very little. Once sent, it's like handing over cash. |

**Never pay a stranger or an unknown shop by bank transfer.** Bank transfers are for paying people you know and trust.

### Lock your money accounts properly

1. Give each bank, shop and PayPal account its **own strong password**, kept in your password manager (Lesson K).
2. Turn on **two-factor authentication** (**2FA**): a second check, such as a code by text or a tap in an app, as well as the password. Look for it in the account's security settings.
3. Never tell anyone a code your bank sends you. Not even someone who says they *are* your bank.

### Check your statements

Once a week, open your banking app and scroll down your recent payments. Look for anything you don't recognise, even small amounts: scammers often test a card with £1 first.

### If money goes missing

Act straight away. Minutes matter.

1. **Ring your bank** using the number on the back of your bank card. Never use a number from a text, email or caller.
2. Or ring **159** from any phone. It's the UK **Stop Scams** number and puts you through to your bank safely.
3. Tell them what happened. They can freeze your card and try to stop or recover payments.
4. Report it to **Report Fraud** (it replaced Action Fraud in 2025), at `reportfraud.police.uk` or on **0300 123 2040** (in Scotland, ring Police Scotland on **101**).
5. Change the password for that account (Lesson K).

## Do it now

1. Open your banking app, or sign in to your bank's website by typing its address yourself.
2. Find your security settings and check that two-factor authentication (sometimes called **two-step verification**) is on.
3. Look through the last month of your statement. Check every payment is one you recognise.
4. Turn your bank card over. Save the phone number on the back into your phone's contacts as "Bank (real number)". Add **159** as "Stop Scams".

You've done it when both numbers are in your contacts and you've checked a month of payments.

## Scribble watch

The Scribble rings up, calls itself "your bank's fraud team", and says your money is in danger and must be moved to a "safe account". There is no such thing. A real bank will never ask you to move money, or tell you a code. Hang up. Wait five minutes (or use a different phone), then ring **159** or the number on your card.
