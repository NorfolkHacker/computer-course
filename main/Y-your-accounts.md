## The Operator says

I am, as you know, the keeper of everyone's passwords at work. People think this makes me powerful. It does. But even I can't get you back into your Google account. Nobody can, except you, and only if you set it up beforehand.

Your **Microsoft account** and **Google account** are master keys. Lose one and you lose your email, your photos, your phone's backups, your files, and every other account that sends its "reset your password" emails there. Ten minutes today, while you can still get in, saves weeks of misery later. Do it now. I'll wait. I have a long list of other people to ignore.

## The Mender's way

Your accounts are the keys to everything else. Make sure each one has up-to-date ways to prove it's you, and that you've written them down.

### What these accounts are

| Account | What it unlocks | Usually looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Microsoft account** | Signing in to Windows, **Outlook** email, **OneDrive** files, Office | An email address ending in `@outlook.com` or `@hotmail.com`, or any email you registered |
| **Google account** | Your Android phone, **Gmail**, **Google Photos**, the **Play Store**, phone backups | An address ending in `@gmail.com` |

You might have both. Most people with a Windows PC and an Android phone do.

### Recovery details

**Recovery details** are how the company checks it's really you if you forget your password or get locked out:

- A **recovery phone number**: your current mobile number, for a text with a code.
- A **recovery email**: a *different* email address you can still get into.

If either is out of date (an old phone, an old work email), fix it today.

### Checking your Google account

1. On your computer, open the browser and go to `myaccount.google.com/security-checkup`. Sign in if asked.
2. The **Security Checkup** shows a list with green ticks or yellow warnings. Work through anything that isn't green.
3. Next, go to `myaccount.google.com/security`.
4. Under **How you sign in to Google**, check **Recovery phone** and **Recovery email**. Click each to correct it.
5. Check **2-Step Verification** is **On** (Lesson K).

On your Android phone, you can do the same from **Settings** → **Google** → **Manage your Google Account**, then the **Security** tab.

### Checking your Microsoft account

1. Go to `account.microsoft.com/security` and sign in.
2. Look for the section listing the ways you sign in and prove who you are (Microsoft changes the wording from time to time; it's currently called **Manage how I sign in**; older pages say **Advanced security options**).
3. Check the phone number and email listed are current. Add your mobile and another email if they're missing; remove any old ones.
4. Under **Additional security**, turn on **Two-step verification** if it's off.

To see which Microsoft account you use on your PC: **Start** → **Settings** → **Accounts**. Your email address is at the top.

### Never share verification codes

A **verification code** is the short number (often six digits) a company texts or emails you to prove it's you. It's a key to your account.

**Nobody genuine will ever ask you to read it back to them.** Not Google, not Microsoft, not your bank, not a friend. Anyone asking for one is trying to break in.

### If you're locked out

1. Go to the sign-in page and click **Forgot password?**
2. Follow the steps. It will send a code to your recovery phone or email.
3. If those are out of date, Google will ask questions to try to confirm it's you. Microsoft has an **account recovery form**. Use a computer or phone you've signed in on before, at home, and answer as fully as you can.
4. Be patient; it can take a few days.
5. Only ever use the real sign-in pages (`accounts.google.com`, `account.microsoft.com`). Never pay someone who offers to "recover your account". They are all scammers.

### Write it down, keep it offline

On paper, write down:

- Your Google and Microsoft account email addresses.
- The recovery phone number and recovery email for each.
- Any **backup codes** (Google) or **recovery code** (Microsoft) the security page gives you: one-off codes for when you can't get a text.

Put the paper somewhere safe at home, with your important documents. Not in your wallet, not in a photo on your phone, and not in an email to yourself.

## Do it now

1. Run Google's **Security Checkup** at `myaccount.google.com/security-checkup` and fix anything yellow.
2. Check your Google **Recovery phone** and **Recovery email** are current.
3. At `account.microsoft.com/security`, check your Microsoft account's phone and email are current.
4. Write both accounts' recovery details on paper and put it with your important documents.

You've done it when both accounts show your current mobile number, and the paper is filed away.

## Scribble watch

The Scribble posts helpful replies under complaints online: "I recovered my account with the help of AccountFixer, message them!" It's always a scam: they take your money, then your account. Only Google and Microsoft can recover your account, through their own sign-in pages, and they never charge. If someone offers to help for a fee, block them and walk away.
