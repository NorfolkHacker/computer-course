## The Operator says

Luser list, entry fifteen: "the computer deleted my letter". No. The computer asked you, politely, and you said "Don't Save". I read that list at Christmas.

Most of what you'll ever make on a computer is a document: a letter, a list, a form. Typing is the easy part. Knowing where the file went, what it's called and how to get it back is the part that saves your Saturday. And mine.

## The Mender's way

A **document** is a file with writing in it. You write it in a **word processor**: an app for typing and arranging words, such as **Microsoft Word** or **Google Docs**. The ideas are the same in both.

### Which one to use

| App | Cost | Where your documents are kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Word** (part of **Microsoft 365**) | Paid subscription, sometimes already on the PC | On your PC or in **OneDrive** (Microsoft's online storage) |
| **Word for the web** | Free with a Microsoft account | In OneDrive |
| **Google Docs** | Free with a Google account | In **Google Drive** (Google's online storage) |

To use the free ones, open your web browser and go to **office.com** (for Word) or **docs.google.com** (for Google Docs), and sign in.

### Starting a document

1. **Word:** click **Start**, type `Word`, and open it. Click **Blank document**.
2. **Google Docs:** at **docs.google.com**, click **Blank document** (the big white square with a coloured plus sign).

A white page appears with a flashing line, the **cursor**, showing where the next letter will go. Just start typing. The words wrap onto the next line by themselves; press <kbd>Enter</kbd> only to start a new paragraph.

### Selecting text

To change words, first **select** (highlight) them, so the computer knows which ones you mean.

1. Put the mouse pointer just before the first word.
2. Hold the left mouse button down and drag to the end of the words.
3. Let go. The words now have a coloured background. They're selected.

Double-click a word to select just that word. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>A</kbd> selects everything.

### Bold and size

With the words selected:

1. To make them **bold** (thicker), click the **B** button in the toolbar at the top, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>B</kbd>.
2. To make them bigger or smaller, find the box with a number in it (usually **11** or **12**) near the **B**. Click the little arrow beside it and choose a bigger number, such as **16**.

### Undo: your best friend

Made a mess? Deleted a paragraph by accident? Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd>. It **undoes** the last thing you did; press it again to go further back. There's also a curved-arrow **Undo** button at the top. If you undo too far, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Y</kbd> puts it back.

### Saving: name it and know where it went

**Saving** writes your document into a file so you can open it again later.

In **Word** on the PC:

1. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd> (or click **File**, then **Save**).
2. The first time, Word asks for a name. Type a **sensible name**: what it is and when. For example `Letter to council 2026-11`. Not `Document1`. Not `asdf`.
3. Look at where it's going (**OneDrive**, **Documents** or a folder name). If unsure, choose **Documents**; click **More options** to pick a folder.
4. Click **Save**. The name now shows at the top of the window.

After that, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>S</kbd> saves again silently, same name, same place. Press it often.

**Word for the web** and **Google Docs** save by themselves. You only need to name the document:

1. **Google Docs:** click **Untitled document** at the top-left and type a name. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
2. **Word for the web:** click the name at the top (something like **Document**) and type a new one.

### Save vs Save As

- **Save** keeps the same name and place, and replaces the old version with the new one.
- **Save As** (in Word: **File**, then **Save As**, or **Save a Copy**) makes a **new, separate copy**, with a new name or in a new place. The original stays as it was.

Use **Save As** to keep the old version, for example when making this year's letter from last year's.

If Word asks **Do you want to save your changes?** when you close it, the safe answer is almost always **Save**.

### Opening it again

1. **Word:** open Word. Recent documents are listed on the first screen.
2. **File Explorer:** open **Documents** and double-click the file (Lesson E).
3. **Google Docs:** go to **docs.google.com**; your documents are listed there.

## Do it now

1. Open Word (or Word for the web, or Google Docs) and start a **Blank document**.
2. Type two sentences about your breakfast.
3. Select the first sentence and make it **bold** and size **16**.
4. Delete the second sentence, then press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> to bring it back.
5. Save it with the name `Breakfast test`. Notice where it went.
6. Close the document completely. Then open it again from the recent list or from **Documents**.

You've done it when `Breakfast test` is back on screen, bold and all.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: You're closing Word and it asks "Do you want to save your changes?" What's the safe answer?
  options:
    - Don't Save, because the computer keeps everything anyway
    - Save
    - Close the box with the cross and hope
  answer: 1
  why: Save keeps your work. Don't Save throws away everything since you last saved; there is no secret copy.
- q: What's the difference between Save and Save As?
  options:
    - Save keeps the same file; Save As makes a new copy with a new name or place
    - They do exactly the same thing
    - Save As deletes the old file
  answer: 0
  why: Save updates the file you already have. Save As creates a separate copy, leaving the original as it was.
- q: You've just deleted a whole paragraph by mistake. What do you press?
  options:
    - Ctrl + S
    - Ctrl + B
    - Ctrl + Z
    - The power button
  answer: 2
  why: Ctrl + Z undoes the last thing you did, including deleting text. Press it again to go back further.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble loves files called `Document1` and `New Doc (3) FINAL final`. It hides your letter among them until you give up. Every time you save, give the file a name that says what it is and when, and glance at where it's going before you click **Save**.
