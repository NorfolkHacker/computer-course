## The Operator says

Luser list, entry twelve: "Can you get my photos back?" I say "Let me check the backup," and then I enjoy a long, meaningful pause.

Rule one: if it only exists in one place, it doesn't exist. Computers die, phones get dropped in rivers, laptops get left on trains, and tea goes where tea wants. A **backup** is a second copy kept somewhere else, so when one copy goes, you shrug instead of weep. It takes ten minutes to set up and then it looks after itself. Mostly. You still have to check it, which is the bit everyone skips.

## The Mender's way

A **backup** is a spare copy of your files, kept somewhere else. If the computer or phone is lost or broken, the copy is safe and you can get everything back.

### What "the cloud" is

**The cloud** means storage on big computers owned by a company like Microsoft or Google, reached over the internet. Your files are copied there automatically. It isn't in the sky; it's someone else's very well-looked-after computer. You get some free:

| Service | Comes with | Free space |
|---|---|---|
| **OneDrive** | Windows and your Microsoft account | 5 GB |
| **Google Photos / Google Drive** | Android and your Google account | 15 GB (shared with Gmail) |

5 GB is about a thousand or so phone photos. If you need more, both sell extra space (Google's is called **Google One**; Microsoft's comes with **Microsoft 365**).

### On Windows: OneDrive folder backup

**OneDrive** can keep a copy of your **Desktop**, **Documents** and **Pictures** folders in the cloud.

1. Look in the bottom-right of the taskbar for a small **cloud** icon ☁. If you can't see it, click the little **^** arrow there to show hidden icons.
2. Click the cloud icon. If it asks you to sign in, use your Microsoft account.
3. Click the **gear** icon ⚙ (top-right of the panel), then **Settings**.
4. Click **Sync and back up**, then **Manage back up**.
5. Turn on **Desktop**, **Documents** and **Pictures**, then click **Start backup** (or **Save changes**).

Another route: open **Settings** (<kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>I</kbd>), click **Accounts**, then **Windows backup**, and look at **OneDrive folder syncing**.

Once it's on, files in those folders show small status icons: a **green tick** means the file is safely copied.

### On Android: Google Photos and phone backup

**Photos and videos:**

1. Open the **Google Photos** app.
2. Tap your **profile picture** or initial (top-right).
3. If you see **Turn on backup**, tap it. If it says **Backup complete** or shows a tick, it's already on.
4. Choose a quality: **Storage saver** fits more in your free space; **Original quality** keeps full detail.

**Everything else** (contacts, messages, settings, app list):

1. Open **Settings** and tap the search bar at the top.
2. Type `backup` and tap **Backup** (on a Pixel it's under **System** → **Backup**; on other phones it may be under **Google** → **Backup**).
3. Make sure **Backup by Google One** (or just **Backup**) is turned on. Tap **Back up now** to do one straight away.

### An external drive: a second copy at home

The cloud is one copy. For things you really can't lose, keep another on an **external drive**: a small box or stick you plug into a USB socket (Lesson U).

1. Plug the drive into the computer.
2. Open **File Explorer** (the yellow folder on the taskbar).
3. Click **Pictures** (or **Documents**) on the left.
4. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>A</kbd> to select everything, then <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> to copy.
5. Click the drive on the left (under **This PC**, with a name like **USB Drive (E:)**).
6. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> to paste. Wait until the copying window finishes.
7. Before unplugging, right-click the drive and choose **Eject**.

Do it once a month, and keep the drive somewhere other than next to the computer. A drive that sits next to the computer goes under the same mug of tea.

### Checking the backup actually works

A backup you've never looked at is a hope, not a backup.

- **OneDrive:** in your browser, go to `onedrive.live.com`, sign in, and open a recent file.
- **Google Photos:** on the computer, go to `photos.google.com` and look for a photo you took this week.
- **External drive:** open the drive in File Explorer and open a photo from it.

If you can open it from the backup, you can get it back.

## Do it now

1. Turn on OneDrive backup for **Desktop**, **Documents** and **Pictures** on the computer (steps above).
2. Turn on backup in Google Photos on your phone.
3. Take a photo of something nearby with your phone.
4. Wait a few minutes (on Wi-Fi), then on the computer go to `photos.google.com`.

You've done it when you can see that new photo on the computer's screen without having plugged the phone in.

## Scribble watch

The Scribble has a nasty trick called **ransomware**: it locks every file on your computer and demands money to unlock them. Your best defence is a backup it can't reach. Keep your external drive **unplugged** except while you're copying to it. If the Scribble ever gets in, you wipe the computer and copy everything back, and it gets nothing.
