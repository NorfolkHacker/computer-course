## The Operator says

Luser list, entry 2,114: "USB stick is upside down". It is always upside down. It is upside down the first time and the second time. On the third try it goes in. This is a law of physics and I have stopped arguing with it.

But here's the serious bit. Plugging things in is how your photos get off your phone, how your files get to the printer shop, and how your headphones talk to your computer. Pull a stick out at the wrong moment and the file you just "copied" may be half a file. Do it properly and it takes three extra seconds.

## The Mender's way

Things connect to a computer or phone in two ways: with a cable (**USB**) or without one (**Bluetooth**). Here is how to do both safely.

### USB plugs

**USB** is the standard plug for connecting things: memory sticks, phones, keyboards, chargers. There are two shapes you'll meet:

| Plug | What it looks like | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **USB-A** | Flat and rectangular, about 1 cm wide | Only goes in one way up. If it doesn't fit, turn it over. Never force it. |
| **USB-C** | Small, thin, rounded oval | Goes in either way up. Used by almost all new Android phones and many laptops. |

A **memory stick** (also called a USB stick or flash drive) is a small plug-in drive for carrying files from one computer to another.

### Copying a file to a memory stick

1. Push the memory stick gently into a USB socket on the computer.
2. Wait a few seconds. A box may pop up asking what to do; you can ignore it.
3. Open **File Explorer**: the yellow folder icon in the taskbar, or press <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>E</kbd>.
4. Find the file you want to copy. Click it once to select it.
5. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> to copy it.
6. In the left-hand column of File Explorer, click **This PC**. Your memory stick appears under **Devices and drives**, often called **USB Drive** or the maker's name. Double-click it.
7. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> to paste. Wait until any progress box has closed.

### Safely ejecting

**Ejecting** tells the computer to finish writing to the stick before you pull it out.

1. In File Explorer, right-click the memory stick in the left-hand column (under **This PC**).
2. Click **Eject**.
3. Wait for the message saying it is safe to remove the hardware, then pull the stick out.

### Bluetooth: pairing headphones or a speaker

**Bluetooth** is a short-range wireless connection, for things within a few metres. **Pairing** is introducing two devices to each other the first time; after that, they connect by themselves.

First, put the headphones or speaker into **pairing mode**. This is usually done by holding its power button or a button marked with the Bluetooth symbol until a light flashes. Check its instructions; every make is different.

### On Windows

1. Click **Start**, then **Settings**.
2. Click **Bluetooth & devices** on the left.
3. Make sure the **Bluetooth** switch is **On**.
4. Click **Add device**, then click **Bluetooth**.
5. Wait for your headphones to appear in the list, then click their name.
6. When it says **Your device is ready to go!**, click **Done**.

### On Android

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Connected devices**, then **Pair new device**. (On Samsung phones: **Connections**, then **Bluetooth**. If you can't find it, use the search bar at the top of **Settings** and type *Bluetooth*.)
3. Wait for your headphones to appear, then tap their name.
4. If asked to confirm pairing, tap **Pair**.

### Copying photos from your phone to the computer

1. Unlock your phone.
2. Plug the phone into the computer with a USB cable. Some cheap cables only charge; if nothing happens at all, try the cable that came with the phone.
3. Pull down from the top of the phone screen to see your notifications. Tap the one that says something like **Charging this device via USB**.
4. Under **Use USB for**, tap **File transfer**. (Samsung phones may instead show **Allow access to phone data?**; tap **Allow**.)
5. On the computer, open **File Explorer** and click **This PC**. Your phone appears by name. Double-click it.
6. Double-click **Internal shared storage** (or **Phone**), then **DCIM**, then **Camera**. Your photos are here.
7. Select the photos you want, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd>, open your **Pictures** folder, and press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd>.

### Chargers

- Use the charger that came with the device, or a good-quality one from a known make.
- Very cheap unbranded chargers from market stalls and odd websites can overheat. Not worth it.
- Don't leave a phone charging under a pillow or on a bed.

## Do it now

1. Plug your phone into the computer with its cable.
2. Switch the phone to **File transfer**.
3. In **File Explorer**, open your phone, then **DCIM**, then **Camera**.
4. Copy one photo into your computer's **Pictures** folder.
5. Unplug the phone.

You've done it when that photo opens from the **Pictures** folder on the computer with the phone unplugged.

## Scribble watch

The Scribble leaves memory sticks lying around: in car parks, on trains, in envelopes marked "Photos". Curiosity plugs them in, and the Scribble walks straight into your computer. Never plug in a memory stick you found or were sent by someone you don't know. Hand it in or bin it. Also, never use public USB charging sockets for a phone that's unlocked; use your own plug and charger.
