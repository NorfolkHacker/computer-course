The keyboard is how you write words, fill in forms and type passwords. Passwords are fussy: one wrong capital and the door stays shut. So: which key does what, where your typing lands, and how to fix a mistake. Slowly. Fast comes later, by itself.

## The Mender's way

You don't need to type quickly. Practise in **Notepad**, a simple app for typing plain text: click **Start**, type `notepad`, and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

### The cursor: where your typing goes

When you click in a box where you can type, a thin upright line blinks there. That's the **cursor**. Letters always appear at the cursor. If your typing isn't appearing, click inside the box first so the cursor is there.

The cursor is not the same as the mouse pointer. The pointer is the mouse's arrow; the cursor is the blinking line. Click in text to move the cursor there.

### The keys you need

| Key | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Letters and numbers | The middle | Letters come out small (lower case). |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> | Two of them, left and right, marked ⇧ | Hold it down while you press a letter to get one capital letter. Also gives the top symbol on a key (see below). |
| <kbd>Caps Lock</kbd> | Left side, above the left <kbd>Shift</kbd> | Press once and *every* letter comes out in capitals until you press it again. A small light shows when it's on. |
| <kbd>Space</kbd> | The long bar at the bottom | Puts a gap between words. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Right side, marked ↵ | Starts a new line; in a sign-in box, it means "go". |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | Above <kbd>Enter</kbd>, often marked ⟵ | Deletes the character to the **left** of the cursor. |
| <kbd>Delete</kbd> (<kbd>Del</kbd>) | Usually in a small group to the right | Deletes the character to the **right** of the cursor. |
| Arrow keys ← ↑ → ↓ | Bottom right | Move the cursor without deleting anything. |

### Fixing a mistake

1. Use the arrow keys (or click) to put the cursor just after the mistake.
2. Press <kbd>Backspace</kbd> once for each wrong letter.
3. Type the right letters.

Holding a key down makes it repeat, so tap <kbd>Backspace</kbd> rather than holding it.

### Symbols

Many keys show two things. For the **top** one, hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> and press the key. So <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>1</kbd> gives `!`, and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>3</kbd> gives `£` on a UK keyboard.

The **@** sign (say "at"), which every email address needs: on a **UK keyboard**, it's on the key just right of <kbd>;</kbd>, with `'` underneath. Hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> and press that key. If your keyboard shows `@` above the <kbd>2</kbd> instead, it's a US-style keyboard: use <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>2</kbd>.

Before typing a password, glance at the <kbd>Caps Lock</kbd> light. If it's on, press <kbd>Caps Lock</kbd> once to turn it off.

### On Android (your phone)

The phone's keyboard appears on screen whenever you tap in a box where you can type. Most phones use **Gboard** (Google's) or **Samsung Keyboard**; both look much the same.

- **One capital letter**: tap the **Shift** key (an upward arrow ⇧, bottom-left of the letters), then the letter.
- **All capitals** (like Caps Lock): double-tap the **Shift** key. Tap it again to turn it off.
- **Numbers and symbols**: tap the **?123** key (bottom-left). For more symbols, tap **=\<** on that page. Tap **ABC** to go back to letters. On many phones, **@** is on the **?123** page; when you're typing in an email address box, it's often right on the main keyboard.
- **Backspace**: the key with a ⌫ (arrow with a cross), right side above the bottom row.
- **Enter**: bottom-right. It may show ↵, a tick, a magnifying glass or an arrow.
- **Moving the cursor**: tap where you want it in the text.
- **Hiding the keyboard**: use your phone's back gesture or back button.

## Do it now

1. Open **Notepad** (**Start**, type `notepad`, press <kbd>Enter</kbd>).
2. Type your first name with a capital letter at the start, using <kbd>Shift</kbd>.
3. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to start a new line. Type a made-up email address, such as `psych@example.com`, using the **@** key.
4. Use the ← arrow key to move the cursor back into the middle of a word. Press <kbd>Backspace</kbd> to delete one letter, then type it back in.
5. Press <kbd>Caps Lock</kbd>, type `HELLO`, then press <kbd>Caps Lock</kbd> again so the light goes off.
6. Close **Notepad** with the **X** in its top-right corner. If it asks whether to save, click **Don't save**.

You've done it when you've typed a capital letter, an @ sign and a correction without looking anything up.

## Quiz

```quiz
- q: Your password keeps being "incorrect" and you're sure it's right. What should you check first?
  options:
    - Whether the Caps Lock light is on
    - Whether you're pressing the keys hard enough
    - Whether the keyboard needs replacing
  answer: 0
  why: If Caps Lock is on, every letter comes out as a capital and the password won't match. Press Caps Lock once to turn it off and try again.
- q: Which key deletes the letter just to the LEFT of the cursor?
  options:
    - Delete
    - Backspace
    - Enter
    - Space
  answer: 1
  why: Backspace removes the character before the cursor. Delete removes the one after it.
- q: How do you type the symbol printed at the TOP of a key, such as ! on the 1 key?
  options:
    - Press the key twice
    - Turn on Caps Lock
    - Hold Shift and press the key
  answer: 2
  why: Shift gives you the top symbol on a key. Caps Lock only affects letters, not symbols or numbers.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble's favourite trick is watching for passwords typed where they don't belong. Habit: before you type a password, check **where the cursor is**. It should be blinking in a box marked **Password**, on a page or app you opened yourself. Never type a password into an email, a chat message or a pop-up that appeared on its own. If in doubt, close it and open the real app or website yourself.
