## The Operator says

Luser list, entry four: a desktop with four hundred icons on it and a sticky note on the monitor saying "don't delete anything". I didn't delete anything. I just stood there, respecting it.

Everything you keep on a computer, every letter, photo and form, is a **file**. Files you can't find are files you've effectively lost. Folders are how you keep them findable, and the Recycle Bin is how you get them back when your hand slips. Learn both now, while there are only a few things to tidy. Later there will be thousands.

## The Mender's way

A **file** is one thing you've saved: a photo, a letter, a PDF. A **folder** is a container that holds files (and other folders), like a cardboard folder in a filing cabinet. On Windows you look at them in **File Explorer**.

### Opening File Explorer

1. Click the **File Explorer** icon on the taskbar: a yellow folder. Or press <kbd>Windows</kbd> + <kbd>E</kbd>.
2. The left-hand column (the **navigation pane**) lists the main places. Click one to see what's in it on the right.

| Place | What goes there |
|---|---|
| **Documents** | Letters, forms, spreadsheets |
| **Pictures** | Photos and images |
| **Downloads** | Anything you download from the internet or save from an email |
| **Desktop** | Whatever is on your desktop screen |
| **Recycle Bin** | Things you've deleted (on the desktop, not in this list by default) |

If your computer uses **OneDrive** (Microsoft's online storage, Lesson L), Documents and Pictures may be inside a folder called **OneDrive**. They work the same way.

To open a file or folder, double-click it. To go back, click the ← arrow at the top left of the window.

### Making a folder

1. Go to where you want the folder, for example **Documents**.
2. Click **New** at the top left of the window, then **Folder**. (Or right-click an empty space, then **New**, then **Folder**.)
3. A folder appears, called **New folder**, with its name highlighted in blue. Type a proper name straight away, such as `Letters`, and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

### Renaming

1. Click the file or folder once to select it.
2. Press <kbd>F2</kbd> (top row of the keyboard), or right-click it and choose **Rename** (in Windows 11 it may be shown as a small icon along the top of the menu; point at the icons to see their names).
3. Type the new name and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Leave any ending like `.pdf` alone.

### Moving and copying

**Moving** puts a file somewhere else. **Copying** makes a second identical file and leaves the first where it was.

To move:

1. Right-click the file and choose **Cut** (or click it and press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>X</kbd>).
2. Open the folder you want it in.
3. Right-click an empty space and choose **Paste** (or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd>).

To copy, do the same but choose **Copy** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd>) in step 1.

You can also drag a file onto a folder to move it there. Watch the label that appears before you let go: it says **Move to** or **Copy to** and the folder's name.

### Deleting, and getting it back

1. Click the file once, then press <kbd>Delete</kbd> (or right-click and choose **Delete**). It goes to the **Recycle Bin**. It isn't gone yet.
2. To get it back, double-click **Recycle Bin** on the desktop.
3. Right-click the file and choose **Restore**. (If you don't see it, click **Show more options** first.) It goes back to exactly where it was.

Emptying the Recycle Bin deletes things for good. Only do it when you're sure.

### On Android (your phone)

On a Google Pixel and many other phones, the app is called **Files** (Files by Google). On a Samsung it's **My Files**. The names below are the Google ones; Samsung's are similar.

1. Open **Files** and tap **Browse** at the bottom.
2. **Categories** such as **Downloads**, **Images** and **Documents** gather files by type. Tap **Internal storage** to see the actual folders.
3. To act on a file, touch and hold it until a tick appears, then tap the ⋮ (three dots) at the top right for **Rename**, **Move to** or **Copy to**.
4. To delete, select it and tap the bin icon. Deleted files go to **Trash** (on the **Browse** page) and stay there for 30 days, where you can restore them.

## Do it now

1. Open **File Explorer** and click **Documents**.
2. Make a new folder called `Practice`.
3. Double-click into **Practice**. Right-click an empty space, choose **New**, then **Text Document**. Name it `test` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
4. Rename `test` to `my first file`.
5. Delete it with <kbd>Delete</kbd>. Open the **Recycle Bin** and **Restore** it.
6. Check it's back inside **Practice**.

You've done it when `my first file` is sitting safely in **Documents** → **Practice**, having been to the bin and back.

```quiz
    - Leave it; you'll remember what's in it
    - Make another one in case
    - Type a proper name straight away, or rename it with F2
  answer: 2
  why: A folder named for what's inside it is easy to find later. Three folders called "New folder" are not.
- q: What is the difference between moving and copying a file?
  options:
    - There is no difference
    - Moving puts it somewhere else; copying leaves the original and makes a second one
    - Copying deletes the original
  answer: 1
  why: Cut and Paste moves a file. Copy and Paste makes a duplicate and keeps the original where it was.
```

## Scribble watch

The Scribble thrives in clutter: a file called "invoice.pdf.exe" hides very nicely among four hundred icons. Habit: keep your things in **Documents** and **Pictures**, in folders with sensible names, and look properly at anything new that appears in **Downloads**. If you don't recognise a file and didn't download it on purpose, don't open it. Delete it.
